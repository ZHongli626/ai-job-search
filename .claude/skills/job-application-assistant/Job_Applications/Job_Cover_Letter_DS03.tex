\textbf{Application for Quantitative Research Analyst -- Track B: Machine-Learning Signals} \\

\vspace{0.05cm}

\noindent Dear Recruiting Team,

I am currently completing my Ph.D.\ in Economics at Bielefeld University, with planned completion in September 2026. My research combines machine learning, statistical modeling, financial data analysis, simulation, and empirical validation. The Quantitative Research Analyst (Track B) closely matches my interest in developing economically meaningful predictive signals from financial, alternative, and textual data.

My strongest fit for this role comes from my work on financial fraud detection using supervised machine-learning methods. In this project, I construct firm-level financial features, process heterogeneous accounting and market-related indicators, and train predictive models to detect structured anomalies in financial statements, with a focus on firms targeted by activist short sellers. I evaluate model performance using recall, F1 score, predictive accuracy, and feature importance, while interpreting algorithmic signals in relation to investor protection and market transparency. 
Moreover, across my doctoral research, I have developed end-to-end analytical workflows for complex economic and financial questions. This includes data collection, cleaning, validation, feature engineering, exploratory analysis, model estimation, robustness checks, and the preparation of structured research outputs. In one dissertation project, I estimate an agent-based model of competition and investment dynamics using financial and operational data from AT\&T and T-Mobile. This work required scalable analytical pipelines, simulation-based estimation, counterfactual analysis, and careful evaluation of model assumptions. 

My technical background is well aligned with the requirements of the machine-learning signals track. I have strong experience in Python, and I am also familiar with R and Stata, and I use SQL for analytical querying, joins, aggregation, and data validation. I am particularly interested in developing my skills further in point-in-time equity datasets, cross-sectional return prediction, signal validation, and portfolio-relevant performance evaluation. I understand the importance of avoiding look-ahead bias, testing out-of-sample robustness, monitoring signal decay, and evaluating whether a signal adds genuinely diversifying information beyond existing factor libraries.

Beyond technical skills, I bring intellectual curiosity, attention to detail, and strong communication skills. My academic work has required me to explain complex quantitative findings through papers, presentations, reports, and visualizations for different audiences. I value reproducible workflows, careful documentation, and honest evaluation of model limitations. I would be highly motivated to contribute to the development and diversification of machine-learning signal libraries while learning from experienced quantitative researchers in a rigorous investment environment.

Thank you so much for considering my application! I would welcome the opportunity to discuss how my background and abilities can contribute to the machine-learning signals research effort.
