My teaching philosophy centers on clarity, inclusion, and interaction. I help students build analytical confidence by connecting abstract economic concepts to real-world applications through clear explanations and interactive exercises. By demonstrating how data and code make complex mechanisms tangible, I cultivate both intuition and technical competence.

I taught Applied Empirical Methods and Applied Business Modelling at UZH (2019-2020) and contributed to SYLVA, an automated scoring system. My responsibilities included developing Mathematica code, testing algorithmic accuracy, and improving course materials. This experience strengthened my understanding of how digital tools enhance instruction and promote fair evaluation.

I believe in creating inclusive learning environments where students feel encouraged to ask questions and diverse perspectives drive discovery. I am eager to develop shared teaching materials, organize workshops on computational methods, and mentor research-interested students. In October 2025, I delivered a public lecture on "AI Agents in Science" at Wissenswerkstadt Bielefeld, introducing agentic workflows and LLMs as tools for economics research and education.

I am prepared to teach undergraduate and graduate courses in microeconomics, industrial organization, and applied econometrics. I plan to integrate computational tools, reproducible research workflows, and data-driven case studies to make economic concepts tangible and relevant, helping students develop technical competence and confidence in applying economic reasoning to real-world challenges.
