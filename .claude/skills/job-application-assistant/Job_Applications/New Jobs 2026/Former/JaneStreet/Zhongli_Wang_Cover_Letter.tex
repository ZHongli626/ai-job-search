and I am looking for a quantitative research role where rigorous modeling, careful empirical testing, and collaborative problem solving sit close to real decisions.

Jane Street interests me because the research culture described in the application process matches how I work. 
I like problems where the first model is rarely the final answer, where asking better questions matters as much as using a more complex method, 
and where researchers need to communicate clearly with people who see the same problem from different angles.

My background is most relevant in three areas:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Research judgment:} I build computational models of strategic behavior, estimate parameters, test assumptions, and revise when the evidence points elsewhere.
    \item \textbf{Python and machine learning:} I use Python for modeling, data analysis, and machine learning, including a published financial fraud detection project using XGBoost.
    \item \textbf{Finance and market intuition:} My M.A. and B.Sc. training covered banking, finance, quantitative finance, and financial engineering, while my Ph.D. research studies competition, network effects, and capacity constraints.
\end{itemize}

I first became interested in Jane Street through its public material on quantitative research and interviews, 
which stood out for the emphasis on clear thinking, intellectual honesty, and learning from mistakes. 
I am currently moving from doctoral research toward industry because I want my modeling and programming work to be tested against live, high-dimensional problems where the standard of evidence is demanding.

Thank you for reviewing my application. 
I would welcome the opportunity to discuss where my research background, financial training, and Python experience could be the strongest fit across Jane Street's teams and offices.
