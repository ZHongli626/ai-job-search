## Explain what the following areas are and what they do

The job description groups the topics broadly into banking, finance and risk, emerging topics, and insurance. The boundaries sometimes overlap because a financial institution needs these functions to work together from the customer transaction through risk control, accounting, and regulatory reporting.

### Banking

#### Retail banking

**What it is:** Retail banking provides financial products and services to individual consumers rather than companies or institutional clients.

**What it does:** It manages customer onboarding, current and savings accounts, deposits, consumer loans, mortgages, cards, transfers, fees, interest, and digital or branch-based customer service. Its systems must process large transaction volumes reliably while supporting identity checks, fraud prevention, data privacy, and regulatory compliance.

**Simple example:** A customer opens a current account, receives a debit card, transfers money, and applies for a personal loan through a mobile app.

#### SME banking

**What it is:** SME banking serves small and medium-sized enterprises, whose needs are more complex than those of individual consumers but usually less bespoke than those of large corporations.

**What it does:** It provides business accounts, working-capital loans, credit lines, equipment finance, payment services, payroll support, cash-flow tools, trade finance, and foreign-exchange services. The bank must assess both the company and often its owners, using financial statements, transaction data, collateral, and business prospects.

**Simple example:** A small manufacturer uses a business account, a credit line to cover seasonal cash-flow needs, and a loan to buy new machinery.

#### Transaction banking

**What it is:** Transaction banking helps companies and financial institutions manage their day-to-day movement of money and working capital.

**What it does:** It supports domestic and cross-border payments, collections, cash and liquidity management, account services, trade finance, and sometimes securities services. The objective is to move money securely and efficiently while giving clients visibility and control over their liquidity.

**Simple example:** An international company uses the bank to collect customer payments in several countries, pay suppliers, and concentrate surplus cash in a central treasury account.

#### Payments and cards

**What it is:** This area provides the infrastructure and processes for electronic payments made through account transfers, debit cards, credit cards, mobile wallets, and other payment instruments.

**What it does:** It handles payment initiation, authorization, fraud screening, clearing, settlement, fees, chargebacks, reconciliation, and regulatory controls. Card operations also cover card issuance, limits, PIN management, merchant acceptance, and dispute handling.

**Simple example:** When a customer pays by card, the system checks the card and available funds, authorizes the transaction, transfers information through the payment network, settles the money, and posts it to the correct accounts.

#### Retail lending

**What it is:** Retail lending is the provision of credit to individuals. It includes unsecured consumer loans as well as secured lending such as mortgages and some forms of asset finance.

**What it does:** It manages the full loan lifecycle: application, affordability and credit assessment, pricing, approval, contract creation, disbursement, repayment schedules, interest calculation, collateral, arrears, restructuring, and collections.

**Simple example:** A bank evaluates a customer's income and credit history, approves a five-year personal loan, disburses the money, and collects monthly principal and interest payments.

#### Asset finance

**What it is:** Asset finance funds the purchase or use of a specific asset, such as a vehicle, machine, or piece of equipment. The asset usually provides security for the financing.

**What it does:** It supports products such as leasing, hire purchase, and equipment loans. The provider values and records the asset, calculates payments, manages ownership and collateral, monitors the contract, and handles end-of-term purchase, return, or repossession.

**Simple example:** A logistics company leases delivery vehicles and pays monthly instalments while the financing company retains legal ownership during the lease.

#### Mortgages

**What it is:** A mortgage is a long-term loan secured by residential or commercial property.

**What it does:** Mortgage operations cover property valuation, borrower affordability, credit approval, collateral registration, interest-rate and repayment calculation, disbursement, servicing, refinancing, arrears, and foreclosure where necessary. The area is highly regulated because of its importance to consumers and bank balance sheets.

**Simple example:** A homebuyer contributes a deposit and borrows the remaining purchase price, with the property serving as collateral for the bank.

#### Commercial banking

**What it is:** Commercial banking serves established mid-sized and large businesses. It typically involves larger exposures, more complex products, and more individualized assessment than retail or SME banking.

**What it does:** It provides corporate accounts, bilateral and syndicated loans, revolving credit facilities, cash management, trade finance, guarantees, foreign exchange, and interest-rate hedging. Relationship managers, credit specialists, and product teams work together to structure solutions around the client's business model and financial position.

**Simple example:** A large industrial company receives a multi-year revolving credit facility, cash-management services, and foreign-exchange hedging from its bank.

#### C4B: Cloud for Banking

**What it is:** In SAP Fioneer's terminology, C4B means **Cloud for Banking**. It is a preconfigured, cloud-based banking platform that brings core banking and related capabilities together.

**What it does:** It gives banks and fintechs a foundation for products such as deposits, payments, finance, and compliance without requiring them to build the entire core technology stack themselves. It is designed to improve time to market, scalability, integration, cost predictability, and regulatory reliability. SAP Fioneer has offered it as a Software-as-a-Service hybrid-cloud solution built on SAP technology.

**Simple example:** A new bank can use C4B to launch accounts and payment services on a regulated, scalable core platform faster than developing every banking component internally.

#### Embedded finance

**What it is:** Embedded finance integrates a regulated financial service directly into a non-financial company's product or customer journey.

**What it does:** Through APIs and partnerships, it allows retailers, marketplaces, mobility platforms, and software providers to offer payments, accounts, lending, insurance, or other financial products at the point of need. A bank or regulated provider normally supplies the financial infrastructure, risk management, and compliance behind the user-facing brand.

**Simple example:** An online marketplace offers sellers working-capital loans inside its own platform, with a partner bank providing the funding and regulated banking processes.

### Finance and risk

#### Bank finance

**What it is:** Bank finance is the internal finance and accounting function of a bank, not lending provided to bank customers.

**What it does:** It converts transactions from banking products into accounting entries and financial positions. It supports subledger and general-ledger accounting, valuation, profit-and-loss measurement, balance-sheet management, reconciliation, consolidation, management reporting, and statutory or regulatory reporting. Its purpose is to give management, auditors, and regulators an accurate view of the bank's financial condition.

**Simple example:** Interest earned on a loan is calculated in the lending system, posted correctly to the accounts, reconciled, and reflected in the bank's income statement and regulatory reports.

#### Credit risk management

**What it is:** Credit risk management controls the possibility that a borrower or counterparty will fail to meet its financial obligations.

**What it does:** It supports customer assessment, scoring or rating, probability-of-default estimation, collateral valuation, credit limits, approval workflows, exposure monitoring, early-warning indicators, expected-credit-loss calculation, stress testing, and regulatory capital reporting. It helps the institution decide whom to lend to, how much, at what price, and under which conditions.

**Simple example:** Before approving a business loan, the bank estimates the borrower's likelihood of default, assesses collateral, checks total exposure, and sets a risk-adjusted interest rate and limit.

#### Insurance finance

**What it is:** Insurance finance is the accounting, valuation, planning, and reporting function for an insurance company.

**What it does:** It records premiums, claims, commissions, investments, expenses, and reinsurance transactions; values insurance liabilities; reconciles operational and accounting data; and produces management, statutory, and regulatory reports. It also supports standards such as IFRS 17 and solvency reporting, depending on the insurer and jurisdiction.

**Simple example:** When an insurer sells a policy and later pays a claim, insurance-finance systems ensure that the premium, liability, claim expense, cash flow, and related reporting are recorded consistently.

### Emerging topics

**What it is:** “Emerging topics” is a broad label for new technologies, regulations, customer expectations, and business models that are changing financial services. It is not one single banking product.

**What it does:** Relevant examples can include generative and agentic AI, open banking and APIs, real-time payments, digital assets, advanced fraud detection, sustainability data, cloud modernization, and new regulatory requirements. Consultants help clients assess the business case, risks, governance needs, operating-model changes, and integration required to move from an idea to a controlled production solution.

**Simple example:** A bank may introduce an AI agent to assist employees with a credit process, but it must restrict data access, respect existing authorizations, log actions, validate outputs, and preserve human approval for material decisions.

### Insurance

#### Property and casualty insurance

**What it is:** Property and casualty insurance, usually abbreviated as P&C or non-life insurance, protects people and organizations against loss or liability involving property, vehicles, accidents, and other non-life risks.

**What it does:** It manages product design, quotations, underwriting, pricing, policy issuance and changes, premium billing, claims, reinsurance, renewals, and cancellations. Underwriting determines whether to accept a risk and at what price, while claims management verifies a covered event and calculates or pays compensation.

**Simple example:** Motor insurance collects a premium in exchange for covering specified accident losses and third-party liability during the policy period.

#### Life insurance

**What it is:** Life insurance provides financial protection or long-term savings benefits connected to a person's life, death, health, or longevity.

**What it does:** It supports products such as term life, whole life, annuities, pensions, and investment-linked policies. Core processes include quotation, medical and financial underwriting, policy administration, premium collection, actuarial valuation, beneficiary management, claims, surrender, and maturity payments.

**Simple example:** A term-life policy pays an agreed benefit to the insured person's beneficiaries if that person dies during the coverage period.

#### Sales and commission management

**What it is:** This area manages how insurance products are distributed and how agents, brokers, banks, and other sales partners are compensated.

**What it does:** It maintains distribution agreements and hierarchies, attributes each sale to the correct intermediary, calculates commissions and bonuses, processes adjustments or clawbacks, creates statements, and supports compliance and performance analysis. Accurate rules are important because insurance products may have long durations and complex commission structures.

**Simple example:** When a broker sells a policy, the system calculates the initial commission, pays recurring commission when applicable, and reclaims part of the payment if the policy is cancelled early.

#### Insurance management

**What it is:** Insurance management is the end-to-end administration and control of an insurer's products, policies, customers, premiums, claims, partners, risk, and operations.

**What it does:** It connects product configuration, underwriting, policy administration, billing and collections, claims, reinsurance, finance, reporting, and customer service. The objective is to operate profitably while honoring policy obligations, managing risk, complying with regulation, and delivering good customer service.

**Simple example:** For a motor policy, the insurer must configure the product, assess the driver, issue and service the contract, collect premiums, process any claim, pay sales commissions, account for all transactions, and report its financial and solvency position.

## Short interview summary

These areas cover the complete value chain of a bank or insurer. The customer-facing businesses, such as retail banking, commercial banking, lending, and insurance sales, create and service financial products. Transaction, payment, policy, and claims systems execute the underlying operations. Credit risk, underwriting, and compliance control the institution's exposure. Bank finance and insurance finance then turn those operational events into accurate accounting, valuation, reconciliation, and reporting. Platforms such as C4B and embedded-finance APIs connect and deliver these capabilities through modern technology. SAP Fioneer's role is to make the whole chain faster, more scalable, more integrated, and easier to control.
