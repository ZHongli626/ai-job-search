Q: Please describe your experience with AI and large language models (LLMs).

A: My experience with AI and LLMs is mainly in building research workflows that combine language-model reasoning with structured data analysis, human review, and reproducible documentation. I am a coauthor of "Agentic Workflows for Economic Research: Design and Implementation", a project that develops an LLM-agent framework for supporting economic research tasks such as literature review, research-question ideation, coding assistance, empirical analysis, and workflow documentation. The implementation uses Microsoft's AutoGen, and my broader tool experience includes LangChain, the OpenAI API, Claude Code, Python, Git, and LaTeX-based research pipelines.

I use LLMs as task-specific assistants rather than black-box decision makers. In my work, this means defining clear agent roles, keeping human-in-the-loop oversight, checking outputs against source material, and maintaining traceable intermediate steps. This approach fits my broader quantitative background in machine learning, statistical modeling, and financial data analysis. For example, my published fraud-detection work used accounting, financial, and market indicators with supervised learning methods such as XGBoost, while my LLM work focuses on making analytical workflows faster, better documented, and more auditable. I am especially interested in applying these methods to financial research, risk analysis, data extraction, and decision-support workflows.
