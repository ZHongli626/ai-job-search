# Decision Proposal: Whether to Accept the PolyU MSc Data Science & Analytics Offer

## Executive recommendation

The offer should be accepted only if the primary objective is a deliberate 1.5-year move into the Hong Kong data/technology market, and if the financial plan can absorb the full cost of study and living without relying on an immediate job offer.

At present, the safer decision is to **wait until the Talent B outcome is clarified**, if the university can grant a short extension or if payment can be made without creating an irreversible financial risk. The pending Talent B is strategically important because leaving Germany for the programme would end the current German residence permit and would reset the prior residence period for future German permanent-residence assessment. The PolyU opportunity is potentially valuable, but it is not a guaranteed immigration or employment route.

If the Talent B is refused, delayed beyond the university deadline, or otherwise cannot provide a credible German path, accepting PolyU becomes more attractive—provided the Hong Kong study and job-search budget is available and the applicant genuinely wants to build a career in Hong Kong.

## Confirmed facts from the available documents

- Programme: MSc Data Science & Analytics at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University.
- The first debit notes require payment by **28 October 2026**, or earlier if required for registration.
- Initial amount currently shown as due:
  - Tuition fee: HKD 116,100
  - Caution money: HKD 400
  - Visa application fee: HKD 700
  - **Total currently shown: HKD 117,200**
- The initial fee is described as non-refundable. The documents do not establish the full tuition amount for the complete 1.5-year programme, so the remaining tuition must be confirmed before accepting.
- The university warns that the student-visa process normally takes **8–10 weeks or longer** during peak periods. The application may be submitted before all conditional-offer conditions are met.
- According to the Bielefeld immigration reply, after completing studies in Germany a job-seeking residence permit must be requested immediately, and a graduation certificate is sufficient to start the process.
- If the applicant leaves Hong Kong for the studies, the German residence permit will expire and earlier residence periods cannot be counted toward the future permanent-residence assessment. Re-entry to Germany as a skilled worker remains possible with a visa.
- A temporary departure generally does not affect pension contributions.

## Main costs of choosing Hong Kong

### 1. Direct financial cost

The immediate committed amount is HKD 117,200, before travel, accommodation, insurance, food, transport, deposits, visa-related administration, equipment, and the remaining tuition instalments. The full budget should include at least:

- all tuition and university charges for 1.5 years;
- rent and housing deposit in Hong Kong;
- food, transport, health insurance and daily expenses;
- flights and relocation/storage costs;
- visa and document costs;
- an emergency reserve;
- the cost of an extended job search after graduation.

The decision should not be based on the first debit note alone. A complete written cost estimate is required before payment.

### 2. Opportunity cost

The programme consumes approximately 1.5 years that could otherwise be used to work, earn income, build German residence history, and continue the German job search. The relevant comparison is not only “tuition versus no tuition”; it is the total Hong Kong cost plus foregone German earnings and residence progress.

### 3. German residence and immigration cost

This is the most important non-financial cost. Leaving Germany for the programme means:

- the current German residence permit expires;
- prior residence periods cannot be used in the future permanent-residence assessment;
- the German post-study job-seeking route described in the reply would no longer be available for this study period;
- returning to Germany would require a new skilled-worker visa route and a qualifying job.

This does not permanently close Germany, but it changes Germany from an existing pathway into a future, conditional pathway.

### 4. Employment uncertainty in Hong Kong

The degree may improve access to Hong Kong data-science roles, but admission does not guarantee a job, sponsorship, or a salary sufficient to recover the investment. The job-search period after graduation must be funded independently of any expected employment outcome.

## Potential benefits of choosing Hong Kong

### 1. A structured route into data science

The MSc provides a concentrated period to strengthen data-science knowledge, build projects, obtain a recognised qualification, and develop a local professional network. This may be particularly useful if the current profile needs a stronger data/ML credential or if German opportunities remain difficult to access.

### 2. Direct access to the Hong Kong market

Studying locally can make it easier to understand employers, attend interviews and networking events, obtain referrals, and demonstrate local availability. These advantages are stronger than applying from Germany, but they still do not remove employment risk.

### 3. Geographic and professional optionality

Hong Kong could become a base for work in Hong Kong, Greater China, or other Asian markets. The programme may also create an international profile that remains useful if the applicant later returns to Germany or another European market.

### 4. A timely alternative while Talent B is pending

The offer provides a concrete alternative rather than leaving the next 1.5 years dependent on an unresolved German immigration or employment outcome. However, this benefit is real only if Hong Kong is itself an acceptable destination—not merely an escape from uncertainty.

## Decision criteria

Accepting the offer is reasonable only if all of the following are true:

1. The applicant wants to live and work in Hong Kong for at least several years, not only study there.
2. The full 1.5-year cost, including living expenses and a post-graduation job-search reserve, is affordable without an immediate job.
3. The applicant accepts losing the current German residence pathway and restarting the residence calculation.
4. The expected career improvement from the MSc is material and specific: target roles, employers, required skills, and a realistic salary range have been identified.
5. The university confirms the complete fee schedule, refund rules, programme dates, visa sponsorship, and any graduate job-search or work rights.
6. The Talent B situation has been resolved, or the decision is being made despite that uncertainty with a consciously accepted downside.

Declining or postponing is preferable if the applicant mainly wants to preserve German residence progress, is uncertain about living in Hong Kong, would need employment to fund the programme, or cannot quantify the full cost.

## Recommended decision process before 28 October 2026

1. Ask PolyU immediately for a short payment extension and request the complete 1.5-year fee schedule in writing.
2. Obtain a written, all-in Hong Kong budget, including a minimum six-month post-graduation job-search reserve.
3. Clarify the Talent B status and the likely decision timeline. Compare the realistic German outcome with the Hong Kong alternative, rather than comparing Hong Kong with an idealised German outcome.
4. Confirm the Hong Kong immigration rules applicable after graduation, including whether and how long the applicant may remain to seek employment and what is required to work.
5. Test the employment thesis: identify target Hong Kong roles, current vacancies, salary ranges, eligibility requirements, and at least a small number of conversations with recruiters or alumni.
6. Do not pay the non-refundable fee until the full budget and immigration consequences are acceptable.

## Final proposal

**Preferred proposal: defer the irreversible commitment briefly while waiting for Talent B clarification, if PolyU permits it.** This preserves the possibility of taking the MSc while avoiding an unnecessary loss of the German pathway before the pending decision is understood.

**Fallback proposal: accept PolyU if Talent B does not provide a credible German route and the full Hong Kong budget is secured.** In that case, the move should be treated as a genuine Hong Kong career strategy, not as a temporary experiment. The applicant should proceed only after accepting that the German residence clock will restart and that returning to Germany later depends on obtaining a qualifying skilled-worker visa.

On the information currently available, the offer is **potentially worthwhile but not yet sufficiently evidenced for an unconditional “yes.”** The decisive questions are the complete programme cost, the applicant’s financial runway, the realistic Hong Kong employment outlook, and the outcome or timing of Talent B.
